## The Retirement Corpus Formula for India

The 4% rule — popular in the US — doesn't work well for India. Here's why:

- **US inflation:** ~3% annually
- **India inflation:** ~6-7% annually (historical average)
- **US healthcare costs:** Mostly covered by Medicare
- **India healthcare costs:** Out-of-pocket (and rising 10-15% yearly)

### The India-Specific Formula

```
Required Corpus = (Monthly Expenses × 12) × 25 × Inflation Multiplier
```

**Inflation Multiplier by Years to Retirement:**

| Years to Retirement | Multiplier | Why |
|--------------------|------------|-----|
| 10 years | 1.8x | ₹1 today = ₹1.80 in 10 years |
| 20 years | 3.2x | ₹1 today = ₹3.20 in 20 years |
| 30 years | 5.7x | ₹1 today = ₹5.70 in 30 years |

**Example:**
- Current monthly expenses: ₹50,000
- Years to retirement: 25
- Inflation multiplier: ~4.3x

**Calculation:**
```
₹50,000 × 12 = ₹6,00,000/year
₹6,00,000 × 25 = ₹1.5 crore (4% rule base)
₹1.5 crore × 4.3 = ₹6.45 crore (inflation-adjusted)
```

**You need ₹6.45 crore.** Not ₹1.5 crore.

This is why most retirement calculators in India are dangerous — they ignore inflation.

## The Inflation Trap: Why Most Indians Get It Wrong

### The ₹2 Crore Mistake

Meet Rajesh, 35, from Mumbai.

He calculates he needs ₹2 crore to retire.

**His math:**
- Monthly expenses: ₹50,000
- ₹50,000 × 12 × 25 = ₹1.5 crore
- Rounds up to ₹2 crore ✅

**Reality at age 60 (25 years later):**
- ₹50,000 in 2026 = ₹2,15,000 in 2051 (at 6% inflation)
- ₹2,15,000 × 12 × 25 = ₹6.45 crore
- His ₹2 crore corpus runs out in **8-10 years**

**At age 70, Rajesh is broke.**

This is not a hypothetical. This is happening to millions of Indians right now.

### The Healthcare Bomb

Healthcare inflation in India: **12-15% annually**

| Age | Likely Medical Expenses (Annual) |
|-----|----------------------------------|
| 60-65 | ₹1 - ₹2 lakhs |
| 65-70 | ₹2 - ₹4 lakhs |
| 70-75 | ₹4 - ₹8 lakhs |
| 75-80 | ₹8 - ₹15 lakhs |
| 80+ | ₹15+ lakhs (with major illness) |

**One cardiac surgery at 70:** ₹3-5 lakhs
**Cancer treatment:** ₹10-25 lakhs

Most retirement plans don't account for this.

## Common Mistakes That Derail Retirement Plans

### Mistake #1: Ignoring Inflation
**Impact:** Underestimate corpus by 60-70%
**Fix:** Use inflation-adjusted calculator

### Mistake #2: Overestimating EPF
**Impact:** EPF covers only 20-30% of needs for most
**Fix:** Track EPF passbook, calculate gap, fill with SIP

### Mistake #3: Starting Too Late
**Impact:** 3x-6x higher monthly investment needed
**Fix:** Start TODAY, even with ₹1,000/month

### Mistake #4: No Healthcare Buffer
**Impact:** Medical emergency wipes out 30-50% of corpus
**Fix:** Add 20% buffer + comprehensive health insurance

### Mistake #5: Ignoring Taxes Post-Retirement
**Impact:** FD interest taxed at slab rate (up to 30%)
**Fix:** Use tax-efficient instruments (SWP from equity, SCSS, POMIS)

### Mistake #6: Not Reviewing the Plan
**Impact:** Market changes, life changes — plan becomes irrelevant
**Fix:** Annual review on your birthday

### Mistake #7: Withdrawing EPF When Changing Jobs
**Impact:** Lose compounding, restart from zero
**Fix:** Transfer EPF, never withdraw

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much money is enough to retire in India?

For a moderate lifestyle in a Tier 1 city, you need **₹5-7 crore** at age 60. For Tier 2 cities, **₹3-4 crore** is sufficient. Use an inflation-adjusted calculator for your exact number.

### Is ₹1 crore enough to retire in India?

**No**, for most people. ₹1 crore generates ₹30,000-40,000/month through SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan). After inflation, this purchasing power erodes rapidly. ₹1 crore might work in Tier 3 cities with frugal lifestyle, but it's risky.

### How much monthly income do I need after retirement?

A good rule: **70-80% of your pre-retirement income**. So if you earn ₹1 lakh/month now, plan for ₹70,000-80,000/month post-retirement. However, account for inflation — ₹70,000 today will need to be ₹2-3 lakhs in 25 years.

### What is the best retirement plan in India?

There's no single "best" plan. The optimal strategy combines:
- **EPF** (employer-matched, tax-free)
- **NPS** (market-linked, extra tax benefit)
- **PPF** (guaranteed returns, tax-free)
- **Equity SIPs** (growth engine)
- **Health insurance** (protection)

### Can I retire at 45 in India?

Yes, but you need aggressive planning. The FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) movement is growing in India. You'll need:
- 25x your annual expenses invested
- Low withdrawal rate (3-3.5%)
- Frugal lifestyle
- Side income or passion project

Use our [FIRE calculator](https://retirepro.in/) to check feasibility.

### What happens if I outlive my retirement corpus?

This is a real risk with increasing life expectancy (85+ years). Solutions:
- Plan for 90+ years
- Keep 20% corpus in annuity plans
- Maintain part-time work option
- Consider reverse mortgage
- Live with children (cultural norm in India)

### How do I generate monthly income after retirement?

**Best options for Indians:**
1. **SWP from mutual funds** (tax-efficient)
2. **Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS)** (8.2%, quarterly payout)
3. **Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS)** (7.4%, monthly)
4. **Pradhan Mantri Vaya Vandana Yojana** (7.4%, pension)
5. **Dividend stocks** (risky but potential)
6. **Rental income** (if you have property)

### Should I buy a retirement plan from an insurance company?

**Be cautious.** Traditional retirement plans from insurers often give 5-6% returns with high charges. You're better off with:
- Direct mutual fund SIPs (12-14% returns)
- NPS (low cost, market-linked)
- Self-directed portfolio

If you want guaranteed income, consider annuity plans from LIC or private insurers — but only for a portion of your corpus.
