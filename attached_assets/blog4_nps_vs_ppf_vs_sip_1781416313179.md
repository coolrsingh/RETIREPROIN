## NPS — The Government's Most Underrated Retirement Scheme

The National Pension System has a reputation problem. It sounds boring, it's associated with government employees, and the mandatory annuity clause puts people off. But if you're in a high income tax bracket, NPS might be the smartest thing you're not using.

**How it actually works:**

You open an NPS Tier 1 account (the retirement account) and choose how your money is invested — across equity (E), corporate bonds (C), and government securities (G). You can go up to 75% equity if you're under 50, which means your money can genuinely grow.

At 60, you can withdraw 60% of the corpus tax-free. The remaining 40% goes into an annuity — a pension-style monthly payout for the rest of your life. This is the part people complain about, and fair enough — annuity rates in India are not great, typically 5–6% per annum.

**The tax advantage is real and significant:**

Under Section 80CCD(1B), you get an additional ₹50,000 deduction over and above the standard ₹1.5 lakh under 80C. If you're in the 30% tax bracket, that's ₹15,000 saved on taxes every year just from NPS — roughly ₹3 lakh over 20 years.

**What returns can you actually expect?**

NPS equity funds have historically delivered around 9–12% CAGR over long periods, closely tracking benchmark indices like Nifty 100. In the 5-year period ending October 2024, NPS equity funds delivered approximately 19–21% CAGR (though that's a particularly strong market run, not a long-term average to count on).

For planning purposes, assuming 10–11% CAGR on an NPS portfolio with 60–70% equity allocation is reasonable.

**Best for:** Salaried professionals in the 20–30% tax bracket who want disciplined, forced long-term savings with meaningful tax savings. Also excellent if you want a built-in "pension" in retirement through the annuity component.

**The catch:** You cannot touch this money easily until 60. If you're someone who might need liquidity in a pinch, NPS's rigidity will frustrate you.

## PPF — The Safe Harbour That Never Lets You Down

The Public Provident Fund is India's most trusted savings instrument. Backed by the Government of India, it has never defaulted, never missed an interest payment, and gives you fully tax-free returns — what's known as EEE status (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt: investment, growth, and withdrawal are all tax-free).

**How it actually works:**

You invest between ₹500 and ₹1.5 lakh per year in your PPF account. The government sets the interest rate every quarter — currently 7.1% per annum, compounded annually. After 15 years, you can withdraw the full corpus, tax-free. You can extend in 5-year blocks after that, with or without further contributions.

**The honest numbers:**

₹1.5 lakh/year (the maximum) invested in PPF for 20 years at 7.1% grows to approximately ₹65 lakh. That's entirely tax-free. Not bad — but not enough if it's your only retirement vehicle.

The 15-year lock-in is both a feature and a flaw. It forces discipline, but life is long and circumstances change.

**What PPF is genuinely good at:**

PPF is your retirement anchor — the guaranteed, zero-risk foundation that will absolutely be there when you need it. In a world where equity markets can fall 40% and NPS values fluctuate, your PPF balance is immune to all of that.

Historical SIP returns in equity mutual funds have delivered 1.5 times what PPF delivers over 15-year periods — but those equity returns are not guaranteed. PPF's 7.1% is.

**Best for:** Conservative investors, anyone within 10 years of retirement who needs to de-risk, and as the "safe" component of any balanced retirement portfolio. Also great for your spouse — both of you can have separate PPF accounts, doubling the ₹1.5 lakh annual limit.

**The catch:** With inflation running at 5–6%, a 7.1% return means your real (inflation-adjusted) return is about 1–2%. PPF preserves wealth. It doesn't create it dramatically. If PPF is your only retirement plan, you'll likely fall short.

## Equity Mutual Fund SIP — The Growth Engine

A Systematic Investment Plan in equity mutual funds is the most flexible, highest-potential retirement tool available to Indian investors. It's also the one that requires the most emotional discipline to stick with.

**How it actually works:**

You set up an auto-debit of a fixed amount every month — say ₹10,000 — into a mutual fund scheme. The fund invests in a diversified portfolio of stocks. Over time, the power of compounding turns consistent small investments into a substantial corpus.

**The real return data:**

Equity mutual fund SIPs in diversified flexi-cap or large-cap funds have historically delivered 12–15% CAGR over 15-year periods. In exceptional periods, top-performing funds have done significantly more — but let's be conservative.

At 12% CAGR:
- ₹10,000/month for 15 years → **~₹50 lakh**
- ₹10,000/month for 20 years → **~₹99 lakh**
- ₹10,000/month for 25 years → **~₹1.9 crore**

At 15% CAGR (optimistic but historically achievable for good funds over 20 years):
- ₹10,000/month for 20 years → **~₹1.5 crore**
- ₹10,000/month for 25 years → **~₹3.3 crore**

The difference between 12% and 15% — just 3 percentage points — over 25 years is the difference between ₹1.9 crore and ₹3.3 crore. This is why fund selection and long-term commitment matter enormously.

**No lock-in (mostly):**

Unlike NPS and PPF, you can redeem equity SIP units anytime. This flexibility is a double-edged sword. The same people who can exit anytime often do — typically right when markets have fallen and their instinct says "cut losses." Then markets recover, they've missed the gains, and they've defeated the purpose entirely.

**ELSS — the tax-saving hybrid:**

Equity Linked Saving Schemes (ELSS) are mutual funds that qualify for ₹1.5 lakh deduction under 80C, with a 3-year lock-in. They give you equity returns with a tax benefit. For many people, starting here makes the most sense — you're forced to stay invested for 3 years, you get a tax break, and you learn that markets do recover.

**Best for:** Anyone with a 10-year-plus investment horizon who can stay disciplined when markets drop. The younger you are, the more SIP should dominate your retirement portfolio.

**The catch:** Markets will fall — sometimes 30%, sometimes 40%. If you've never lived through that as an investor, you don't yet know if you're someone who stays invested or someone who panics. Most people discover they're the second type the hard way.

Let's take one common scenario — a 40-year-old investing ₹10,000 per month across different options until retirement at 60 — and see where they land.

The equity SIP wins on raw corpus — but that NPS number comes with ₹30,000+ in annual tax savings, and the PPF number is completely guaranteed and tax-free.

You're not choosing the biggest number. You're choosing the right combination for your life.

## So Which One Should You Actually Use?

Here's the honest answer: **all three — in the right proportion.**

Not because it's a diplomatic non-answer. Because each of them solves a different problem in your retirement plan.

**NPS solves:** Tax efficiency and forced discipline. The extra ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B) is something no other instrument gives you.

**PPF solves:** Risk-free guaranteed base. It's your retirement floor — the minimum you'll have no matter what the market does.

**Equity SIP solves:** Real wealth creation. The only way to genuinely beat inflation over 20 years and build a corpus that gives you choices is equity.

Here's a practical split for a 38-year-old professional earning ₹1 lakh/month:

| Instrument | Monthly Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| NPS Tier 1 | ₹5,000 | Tax saving + moderate growth |
| PPF | ₹5,000 | Safe, tax-free base |
| Equity SIP (flexi-cap) | ₹10,000 | Long-term wealth creation |
| **Total** | **₹20,000/month** | **Full retirement strategy** |

At these amounts, with reasonable return assumptions, this person builds a corpus of roughly ₹2–2.5 crore by 60. Not ₹8 crore (what they might actually need), but a dramatically better outcome than doing nothing — and a foundation that can be scaled as income grows.

The most important upgrade: increase your SIP by 10% every year. That same ₹10,000 equity SIP, growing 10% annually, doesn't build ₹99 lakh — it builds close to ₹2.5 crore on its own.

## The Age Factor: How This Changes Depending on When You Start

**If you're in your 30s:** Go heavy on equity SIPs. Time is your biggest asset. You can afford volatility. NPS for the tax benefit, PPF for discipline, but equity SIP should be the bulk of your retirement investing.

**If you're in your 40s:** Balance matters more now. Increase the NPS and PPF proportion. Still keep equity SIPs running — you have 15–20 years, which is enough. Don't panic-shift to debt because you feel "too old" for equity.

**If you're in your 50s:** Capital preservation starts mattering. Shift NPS allocation toward more bonds (lower equity %). Ensure PPF is extended. Keep a smaller equity SIP running. Focus on maximising corpus while protecting what you have.

## The Verdict

There is no single winner in the NPS vs PPF vs SIP debate because they're not competing against each other — they're solving different parts of the same problem.

PPF is your safety net. NPS is your tax shelter with growth. Equity SIP is your wealth engine.

Used together, they cover every angle of retirement planning: guaranteed returns, tax efficiency, equity growth, and liquidity. Used in isolation, each one leaves significant gaps.

The worst decision you can make is spending another year debating which is best without starting any of them.

**FAQ Section (add below for SEO):**

**Q: Which is better — NPS or PPF for retirement?**
Both serve different purposes. PPF offers guaranteed, fully tax-free returns (currently 7.1%) with zero risk, while NPS offers market-linked returns of 9–12% with significant tax benefits. For retirement, using both together is more effective than choosing one over the other.

**Q: Is SIP better than PPF for long-term retirement planning?**
For a time horizon of 15+ years, equity SIPs have historically delivered 1.5x or more returns compared to PPF. However, SIP returns are market-linked and not guaranteed, while PPF returns are fixed and risk-free. A combination of both works best.

**Q: Can NPS give 12% returns?**
NPS equity funds have historically delivered 9–12% CAGR over long periods, with some periods of higher returns. The actual return depends on your asset allocation choice (equity vs bonds vs government securities) and market performance. For long-term planning, 10–11% is a reasonable assumption for an equity-heavy NPS portfolio.

**Q: How much should I invest in NPS vs SIP?**
A common starting point: invest in NPS up to the ₹50,000 additional deduction under 80CCD(1B) limit to maximise tax benefits, then direct remaining retirement savings into equity SIPs for growth. As income grows, increase both. Use RetirePro.in's calculator to find the right split for your situation.

**Q: What happens to NPS money at retirement?**
At 60, you can withdraw 60% of your NPS corpus as a tax-free lump sum. The remaining 40% must be used to purchase an annuity — a regular monthly income for the rest of your life. Annuity rates in India are currently modest (5–6%), which is the primary limitation of NPS compared to flexible alternatives.
