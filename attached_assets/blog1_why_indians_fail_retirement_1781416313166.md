Let me tell you about Ramesh.

Ramesh is 52. He works at a mid-sized IT company in Pune, earns a respectable salary, owns a flat (mostly the bank's, but still), and considers himself financially responsible. He has an EPF account, a few LIC policies from the 90s, and a fixed deposit his father-in-law gifted him years ago.

Ask him about his retirement plan and he'll say: *"I'll figure it out."*

He's been saying that since he was 38.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: Ramesh is not an outlier. He is India's retirement story.

A 2024 study by Max Life Insurance found that **93% of Indians over 50 regret not starting retirement planning sooner.** Not 20%. Not 40%. Ninety-three percent. That's nearly every person who's already past the point where they could have made the biggest difference — wishing they had started earlier.

This isn't a niche problem. This is a quiet crisis playing out in homes across the country.

So why does it keep happening? And more importantly — what's the one habit that actually changes the outcome?

## The Retirement Gap Nobody Talks About

India's pension system was ranked among the bottom three globally by the Mercer CFA Global Pension Index 2024, scoring just 43.8 out of 100. Only 5.3% of India's total population is covered by NPS and APY combined. The vast majority of working Indians are essentially on their own when it comes to retirement.

And even those who are covered? The EPF data tells a sobering story.

In 2024–25, out of 52.95 lakh final settlement claims with EPFO, a staggering **95% were premature withdrawals** — people pulling money out just two months after losing a job. Not at retirement. Before retirement. And nearly half of EPFO members have **less than ₹20,000 in their accounts** at the time of final settlement.

Let that sink in. Less than ₹20,000. For retirement.

Meanwhile, India's average life expectancy is now 72 years and rising. If you retire at 60, you need to fund 12 to 15 years of expenses — and that's just the average. Many people live well into their 80s today.

The math is brutal. The planning is almost nonexistent.

## Why Smart, Earning People Still Fall Through the Cracks

Here's what confuses most people: India has millions of educated, earning professionals who understand money. They can explain inflation, talk about equity markets, debate crypto. Yet they retire underprepared.

It's not ignorance. It's something deeper.

**The "I'll Start Next Year" Trap**

Compounding is the most powerful force in personal finance — and the most wasted. At 30, ₹5,000 a month invested at 12% becomes roughly ₹1.76 crore by 60. Start the same SIP at 40 and you get ₹50 lakh. That 10-year delay cost you over ₹1.25 crore. Not because you were irresponsible — just because you waited.

The problem is that "next year" always feels reasonable. You'll start when the home loan EMI reduces. When the kids' school fees drop. When the salary hike comes through. And next year becomes next decade.

**Lifestyle Inflation Is Eating Your Future**

You got a 15% raise. Congratulations. You also upgraded your car, moved to a better apartment, started ordering from fancier restaurants, and enrolled the kids in an international school. Three months later, your bank balance looks exactly the same as before the raise.

This is lifestyle inflation — and it's perfectly normal human behaviour. The problem is that it silently cancels out every income increase you'll ever get, leaving nothing for your 65-year-old self.

**The EPF Illusion**

Millions of Indians believe their EPF will take care of retirement. It won't — at least not entirely.

The EPF interest rate is 8.25% for 2024–25. That's not bad. But consider: someone earning ₹80,000 a month contributing 12% employee share for 25 years might accumulate around ₹60–80 lakh. Sounds decent until you realise that in 25 years, with 6% inflation, your current monthly expense of ₹50,000 will become nearly ₹2.15 lakh per month.

Your EPF corpus at 4% withdrawal rate gives you roughly ₹25,000 to ₹30,000 a month. You'll need six to seven times that.

**Children First, Me Last**

This is perhaps the most emotionally loaded one. Indian parents are wired to sacrifice for their children — education, wedding, housing. And there's deep cultural pride in that.

But here's the hard truth: your child can take an education loan. They can have a simpler wedding. They will find a way. You, at 70, with no corpus and no income, have far fewer options. Nobody talks about this openly, but it needs to be said.

**No Real Number to Aim For**

Ask most people how much they're saving for retirement and they'll say "I invest some amount regularly." Ask them how much they'll need at retirement and they'll give you a blank look.

Saving without a number is like driving without a destination. You're moving, but you don't know if you're going the right way.

## What Retirement Actually Costs in India — The Real Number

Let's do something most financial content avoids: actual math.

Say you're 38 today. Your monthly household expenses are ₹75,000. You plan to retire at 60 — that's 22 years away.

At a modest 6% annual inflation, your ₹75,000 today becomes roughly **₹2.7 lakh per month** at retirement.

To sustain ₹2.7 lakh per month for 25 years of retirement (assuming you live to 85), using a standard 4% withdrawal rate, you'll need a retirement corpus of approximately **₹8 crore.**

Eight crore.

## The Small Habit That Changes Everything

Here's the good news: you don't need to solve all of this at once. You need one habit.

**Automate a monthly SIP — starting today, at whatever amount you can.**

That's it.

Not a complicated strategy. Not expert advice. Not a perfect fund selection. Just start a SIP — ₹2,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000, whatever is honest and sustainable for you — and automate it so it leaves your account on the 1st of every month before you can spend it.

Here's why this one habit is so powerful:

It removes the decision from your hands. You don't need to "remember" to invest. You don't need to "feel ready." The money moves automatically.

It harnesses time. Even a small SIP started today is worth dramatically more than a bigger SIP started five years from now. A ₹5,000/month SIP at 12% for 25 years builds ₹94 lakh. The same SIP for 20 years builds just ₹50 lakh. Five years costs you ₹44 lakh.

It grows with you. Most SIP platforms let you increase your SIP amount once a year. If you increase by just 10% annually — roughly in line with typical salary hikes — the outcome changes dramatically. A ₹5,000 SIP growing 10% annually for 25 years builds roughly ₹2 crore.

The magic isn't the amount. The magic is the consistency.

**Q: How much should I save for retirement in India?**
A financial thumb rule is that your retirement corpus should be 25–30 times your annual expenses at the time of retirement, adjusted for inflation. A 38-year-old spending ₹75,000/month today may need ₹7–8 crore by retirement at 60. Use our free calculator to get your exact number.

**Q: Is EPF enough for retirement in India?**
For most salaried professionals, EPF alone is not sufficient for retirement. While it's a valuable foundation, the typical EPF corpus at retirement covers only a fraction of actual post-retirement expenses — especially after accounting for inflation and longer lifespans. EPF should be supplemented with NPS and equity SIPs.

**Q: At what age should I start retirement planning in India?**
The earlier the better — ideally in your late 20s or early 30s. However, starting at 40 is still far better than starting at 50. The key is to begin with whatever amount you can, automate it, and increase it annually.

**Q: What is the best investment for retirement in India?**
A combination works best: EPF for disciplined forced savings, NPS for tax benefits and moderate growth, and equity mutual fund SIPs for long-term wealth creation. The exact split depends on your age, risk appetite, and how many years you have left until retirement.
