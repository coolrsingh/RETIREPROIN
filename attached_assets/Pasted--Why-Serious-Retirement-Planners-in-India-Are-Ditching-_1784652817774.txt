## The Uncomfortable Truth: Why Most Indians Don't Take Retirement Planning Seriously

India has one of the youngest workforces in the world — and one of the least prepared for old age. There are cultural and structural reasons for this, and being honest about them is the first step.

**"My children will take care of me."** For generations, retirement planning in India was simple: raise children, and they become your pension. That model is collapsing. Nuclear families, children settling abroad, rising costs of living in metros, and longer lifespans mean the joint-family safety net is no longer something you can bank on — literally.

**"EPF and gratuity will be enough."** Salaried Indians often assume their Provident Fund is a retirement plan. It isn't. EPF is a component, not a plan. For most people, the EPF corpus at 60 covers only a fraction of 25–30 years of post-retirement expenses, especially once inflation is accounted for.

**Retirement feels far away, so it loses to everything urgent.** A child's school admission is this month. The home loan EMI is this month. Retirement is "later." The human brain systematically discounts distant problems — behavioural economists call it present bias — and retirement is the biggest victim of it.

**Nobody teaches this.** We learn trigonometry in school but not compounding, inflation, or withdrawal rates. Most Indians encounter retirement math for the first time in their 40s — usually through a pushy insurance agent, which makes them trust the whole subject even less.

**Talking about old age feels inauspicious.** There's a quiet cultural discomfort in planning for a phase of life associated with dependence and decline. So people avoid the topic entirely — and avoidance has a compounding cost of its own.

The result: a generation of well-earning professionals in their 30s and 40s who invest *something*, *somewhere*, without ever knowing the one number that matters — **their target retirement corpus**.

## Why Delaying This Calculation Causes Real, Measurable Pain

Here's the part most people underestimate. Delaying retirement planning doesn't just mean "starting a bit late." Because of compounding, the penalty for delay grows *exponentially*, not linearly.

Consider a target corpus of ₹5 crore by age 60, at a typical equity return assumption:

- **Start at 25** → roughly ₹15,000/month is enough
- **Start at 30** → roughly ₹24,000/month
- **Start at 35** → roughly ₹40,000/month
- **Start at 45** → roughly ₹95,000/month

Starting just ten years late can require **2.5x the monthly investment** for the same outcome. Wait until 45, and the required SIP may exceed what your entire salary can support. At that point, your options shrink to three painful choices: retire later, retire poorer, or take investment risks you can't afford at that age.

And the pain isn't only financial. People who discover their gap late describe the same feelings: anxiety every time markets dip, guilt about past spending, tension in the household about money, and the quiet dread of becoming financially dependent on children — the exact thing they wanted to avoid.

**Knowing your number early is not about fear. It's about freedom.** When you know at 32 that you need ₹6.9 crore and you're on track for ₹4.5 crore, the gap of ₹2.4 crore is a solvable engineering problem — a step-up SIP, a slightly later retirement, a small expense adjustment. Discover the same gap at 52, and it's a crisis.

## Don't Just Google, Fill Two Boxes, and Call It "Planning"

This is the most common mistake serious planners must unlearn.

The typical online retirement calculator asks you two things — your monthly expense and your retirement age — then instantly announces a corpus. It feels like an answer. It's actually a guess dressed up as an answer, because it silently assumes:

- **A fixed life expectancy** (often 75 or 80 — dangerously low for today's 30-year-olds, who may easily live past 85–90 with modern healthcare)
- **One flat inflation rate** applied to everything, when medical inflation in India runs far higher than general inflation
- **A fixed post-retirement return** you never chose
- **Constant expenses forever**, ignoring that healthcare costs rise sharply in later decades
- **No life events** — as if no child will be born, no wedding will happen, no parent will need care

Your retirement corpus must be calculated based on **how long you'll actually live after retiring**, not on a convenient default. Retiring at 58 and living to 90 means funding **32 years** of life with zero salary. A calculator that assumes you'll live to 78 will understate your requirement by decades of expenses — and you'll only find out when it's far too late to fix.

The correct approach is to plan for a realistic, even conservative, life expectancy, and to treat every assumption as something *you* examine and set — not something a website decided for you invisibly.

## Every Child, Every Marriage, Every Big Life Event Rewrites Your Retirement Math

Here's what static, one-time calculations completely miss: **your retirement number is a living number.**

**A child is born.** Your monthly expenses rise for the next 20+ years. Education costs — inflating faster than almost anything else in India — enter your future cashflow. Money that would have gone into your retirement SIP now competes with school fees, and later, college costs that can run into tens of lakhs. One child can shift your required corpus and your achievable savings rate simultaneously — a double impact most people never recalculate for.

**A marriage happens.** Household expenses restructure entirely. Sometimes two incomes now fund one retirement plan (a huge advantage — if you actually plan jointly). Sometimes new dependents and obligations appear. Either way, the numbers you calculated as a single person are obsolete.

**You buy a home.** A 20-year EMI reshapes your investable surplus for two decades — but also potentially removes rent from your post-retirement expenses. Both sides of your equation change at once.

**A parent needs medical care. A career break happens. A big salary jump arrives.** Each of these events materially moves either your target corpus, your saving capacity, or both.

## The 8 Factors Serious Planners Must Get Right

When you sit down with RetirePro, examine these deliberately:

1. **Life expectancy** — plan to 85–90, not 75. Running out of money at 82 is not an acceptable failure mode.
2. **Real monthly expenses** — what your household actually spends, not a rounded guess. Include the invisible ones: insurance premiums, annual travel, festivals, family support.
3. **Inflation — with a medical premium.** General inflation of 6% is a starting point; healthcare inflation runs meaningfully higher, and healthcare becomes a bigger share of spending as you age.
4. **Retirement age — honestly.** Corporate careers in India often end earlier than people plan. If there's a chance you'll stop (or be stopped) at 55, model it.
5. **Pre- vs post-retirement returns.** Your portfolio after 60 should be more conservative — which means lower returns during the longest phase of your plan.
6. **Existing corpus, counted properly.** EPF, PPF, NPS, mutual funds, FDs — consolidate the real number. Exclude your primary home; you can't eat a house.
7. **Income that stops vs expenses that don't.** The core of retirement math: the day salary ends, expenses continue for 25–35 more years.
8. **Life events on the horizon.** Children's education, weddings, parental care — these draw from the same pool and must be planned alongside retirement, not discovered against it.
